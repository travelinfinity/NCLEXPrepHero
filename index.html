A client's potassium is 6.9 mEq/L and the monitor shows tall, peaked T waves. Which order should the nurse carry out first?

15-Second Memory Trick ("C before I"): Peaked T-waves mean the myocardium is irritable and about to arrest. Calcium gluconate (B) puts a protective shield over the heart muscle in 1–3 minutes. Once the heart is safe, give Regular Insulin + Dextrose (C) to push potassium into the cells, and Kayexalate (A) ("K-Exits-Late") to excrete it in stool!

🚨 6. The 5 Deadly 'D' Labs (Stay at Bedside!)

  • 1. Potassium > 6.0 mEq/L: Deadly cardiac arrest risk. Hold K+, attach monitor, give IV Calcium Gluconate FIRST (protects the heart), then Regular Insulin + Dextrose, then remove K+ (binders/dialysis).
  • 2. Arterial pH in the 6.0s (< 7.00): Lethal acidemia. Assess vitals, keep Ambu-bag at bedside, call HCP STAT.
  • 3. PaCO2 in the 60s (≥ 60 mmHg): Acute hypercapnic respiratory failure. Prepare for immediate intubation & mechanical ventilation.
  • 4. PaO2 in the 60s (≤ 60 mmHg): Severe hypoxemic respiratory failure. Apply high-flow O2, prepare to intubate.
  • 5. Platelets < 40,000 /mm³: Spontaneous fatal hemorrhage risk. Strict bleeding precautions & prepare platelet transfusion.
